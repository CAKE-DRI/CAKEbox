    Sprinkles of Knowledge

    Welcome to Sprinkles of Knowledge! This is where you can find quick tips, advice, and experiences from our community. The most helpful ideas are often simple: a practical tip, something that worked, a mistake to avoid, or a small insight from experience.

    💬 Agree on how you communicate
    It can be frustrating to send a message that doesn't get checked. Decide what platform to mainly use (i.e. Slack, email, Mattermost) early to reduce friction and stress.
    📝 Share early thoughts and work
    Don't be nervous about sharing rough drafts or unfinished ideas. They often get better feedback than polished versions shared too late.
    🔥 Don't let the awkwardness marinate
    Small annoyances can become big collaboration blockers. Talk about problems while they're still little adjustable things.
    📱 Leave the tech behind
    Science communication skills can be so important to learn how to distil your knowledge into the key concepts for others. Move away from the technical intricacies and think 'What do they actually need to know?'
    📚 Revisit the plan
    A good collaboration plan is not fixed forever. Check in regularly: are your goals, timelines and ways of working still helping you move forward?
    ✨ Save the coffee chats
    Some of the best ideas appear when you're not staring at a shared document. Make time for the informal conversations where you make friends with your collaborators.
    🔍 Share goals!
    Collaborate with those people who share your same interests
    ✨ Consistency matters more than perfection
    Showing up regularly matters far more than the perfect event, website or schedule. People remember reliability, building the community through real connections.
    📚 The ideal community member
    Your community will not serve everyone - and it shouldnt! Define a specific ideal member and ask “What does X need right now?”
    ✨ Ask everyone for adjustments by default
    Normalise adjustments early in processes so participation needs are expected, not exceptions.
    💬 Accessible participation options
    Offer different ways to contribute, such as written input or async feedback to prevent forcing people from speaking up in large groups.
    🎨 Hybrid is a team sport
    A hybrid collaboration won't magically work itself out. Think carefully about how everyone can join in, not just who happens to be in the room.
    📚 Language matters
    Always explain acronyms, maybe keeping a shared glossary. Then everyone can follow along without having to ask about being included.
    📝 Personas and User Stories
    Personas and user stories can help to ensure software meets users needs, but this can also be used to plan events or recruitment - create personas and situations for different needs or backgrounds, and check your plans against them!
    📌 Emded accessibility
    Ask about accessibility requirements as part of standard sign-ups, so adjustments are expected rather than treated as special cases.
    💬 After the event
    Write down your main takeaways, future you will appreciate it :)
    🌟 Coffee breaks are where ideas happen
    Some of the best collaborations start in queues, not rooms
    ✨ Hybrid is hard - but important!
    Hybrid events can be more difficult or expensive. However, ror many it is a choice between "hybrid or not at all" due to caring responsibilities, disabilities, visas and all kinds of other reasons. Don’t lose those people!
    🎨 Quiet spaces
    Always include a clearly signposted area where attendees can take a break whenever they need.
    📚 Using slide decks for semi structured group discussions
    One person presents their slides but instead of rattling through the lot, stop after each slide and have a group discussion. Best with a smaller engaged audience.
    🎯 Archive your talks
    Upload slides and scripts to Zenodo to get a DOI. It makes your presentations citable, permanent, and easy to reuse or reference later.
    📱 Containers: The Building Blocks
    A container is a lightweight, standalone, executable package that includes everything needed to run a piece of software: code, runtime, libraries, and system tools.
    📱 Deployments: Managing Application Lifecycle
    A deployment is a Kubernetes object that manages a set of pods and ensures the desired number of replicas are running at all times.
    📚 Kubernetes vs Docker
    **Docker**: Runs apps from built images in containers, great for sandboxed environments.
    💬 Lightweight first
    Start with simple solutions (markdown, scripts, templates) before jumping into complex platforms
    📝 Notion for everything!
    I love using Notion to keep all my notes in one place. I recommend keeping a long-term future to do list so nothing gets forgotten, while keeping your day-to-day mental space clear
    📱 Pods: The Smallest Deployable Unit in Kubernetes
    A pod is a group of one or more containers that share storage, network, and a specification for how to run the containers.
    🔍 Tracking time
    Toggl Track helps you see where your time goes. Simple summaries make it easier to spot when work starts taking over.
    ✨ Why Kubernetes?
    **Availability** – Kubernetes spreads containers across nodes.
